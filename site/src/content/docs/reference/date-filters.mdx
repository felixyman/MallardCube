The Date Filters submenu only appears on a pivot field when the metadata says
the field's members are dates. When it does appear and a filter is applied,
Excel sends a specific MDX shape — a subquery over the key attribute — that an
engine has to handle as a filter, not as a slicer. Both halves are measured
below; the first is metadata, the second is a query.

## Why the menu appears — or does not

<Claim id="member-value-per-attribute-hierarchy" />

The consequence is concrete: if the full-date level is only reachable as a
level of a user hierarchy, Excel offers no Date Filters on it, no matter how
correct the dates are. It has to be a **single-level attribute hierarchy of its
own**, with `MEMBER_VALUE` advertised as a date type on its level.

## What Excel sends when a filter is applied

<Claim id="excel-date-filter-subquery" />

The important part for an engine is the subquery: `Filter(<key level>.AllMembers,
(CurrentMember.MemberValue = CDate("…")))`. It selects members by their
`MEMBER_VALUE` — which is why the same metadata that makes the menu appear also
makes the filter work. Applying it to the *outer axis* is what Excel expects:
the drilldown on the columns axis is then reduced to the matching date (plus
`(All)`), and the cells follow.

- The reference's answer to the full subquery is not yet reproduced outside
  Excel: an ADOMD replay of the same MDX returned an empty axis, while the
